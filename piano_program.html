Understanding vi-IV-I-V

This is one of the most popular chord progressions in modern music. Used in pop, rock, worship, EDM, and more. Once you learn it, you can play hundreds of songs.

Nashville Numbers

The Formula

In any major key, the vi-IV-I-V progression uses:

vi 6th minor
IV 4th major
I 1st major
V 5th major

It's a loop: minor → major → major → major. The emotional pull from minor to major creates that bittersweet, nostalgic sound.

Key of C Major

Your Starting Point

In C major, the chords are:

Am vi
F IV
C I
G V

Start here because C major has no sharps or flats. All white keys!

How to Transpose

Play in Any Key

To move this progression to another key:

  1. Find the major scale of your new key
  2. Count: 6th degree = minor, 4th = major, 1st = major, 5th = major
  3. Example G major: Em (vi) - C (IV) - G (I) - D (V)

Practice Tips & Style Guide

Make these 4 chords sound different for every artist and genre

🎤

Sia / Bruno / Aitana Style

Ballad Pop: Play broken chords in right hand: 1-5-8-5 pattern. Slow tempo 65-75 BPM. Heavy sustain pedal. Let chords ring. For Bruno's "When I Was Your Man", add grace notes and rubato timing.

⛪

Hymns Hack

Worship Style: Play block chords on beat 1, hold for full measure. Add the 2nd scale degree for "sus" sound: Csus2, Fsus2. In 3/4 time, play: bass note on 1, chord on 2-3. Makes it sound ancient and sacred.

🎧

Alan Walker / Becky G Groove

EDM/Latin Pop: Don't play on beat 1! Play staccato chords on beats 2 and 4 only, or off-beats "and of 1, and of 2". Left hand: simple quarter-note bass C-A-F-G. This creates dance groove space.

🎹

Left Hand Secrets

Level Up Your LH: Week 1-4: Single bass notes. Week 5-8: Octaves C-C A-A F-F G-G. Week 9-12: Add the 5th: C-G, A-E, F-C, G-D. This is called "power chord" bass and sounds huge.
